Results

209 enquiries that never reached the CRM — found and restored.

In October 2026 we ran the intake audit on our own group's websites. An email gateway that turns website enquiries into CRM leads had stopped working in April without any alert. Here is what the audit found and what we did.

Case: our own intake chain, April–October 2026

Measured on our own websites and CRM. Client results will be published here only with the client's written permission.

5,709 inbound emails re-checked

The silent gap

From 1 April 2026 the parser behind the email gateway hit its quota, and every enquiry email was stored as invalid. From April to August the CRM received one lead from these sites, against 90–124 a month before.

209 real enquiries recovered

Six websites

Each email was screened for spam, tests, duplicates and bounces. 209 real enquiries from six websites went into the CRM with their original dates, tagged for follow-up.

5,500 emails filtered out

Noise removed

4,935 came from a single flood on one day, 223 were spam, 65 were duplicates inside the batch, 19 already existed in the CRM, 30 were bounces; the rest were internal tests and emails the newer form hook had already delivered.

What we changed

Fixes in the chain

  • Replaced the failed parser with a new model-based parser that also flags spam and test messages.
  • Moved the gateway to its own database user with only the rights it needs.
  • On the main websites, forms now create the CRM lead directly, and the email gateway is the second path.
Why it matters

The traffic was there

The websites kept getting enquiries for months. Nobody saw them because the step between the inbox and the CRM failed quietly. This is the kind of leak the Revenue Leak Map looks for first: places where an enquiry arrives and no person ever sees it.
