New FCC opt-out rules take effect November 25, 2026: what changes for business texts and AI calls

On November 25, 2026, amended U.S. robocall rules take effect. The FCC order (FCC 26-67) lets callers and text senders designate one exclusive way to opt out, such as replying STOP, as long as they disclose it in each call or text. An opt-out from one type of informational message can now apply to that type only. Every opt-out must still be honored within 10 business days. The rules cover calls with an artificial or prerecorded voice, which includes AI-generated voices.

Published November 6, 2026 · 4 min read

What to do before November 25: if you text or call U.S. numbers with automation, choose your opt-out method, put STOP (or your chosen method) in every message, accept all seven standardized opt-out words, and make sure an opt-out stops the automation well within 10 business days. Ask counsel how the rules apply to your messages.

What the FCC decided

The FCC adopted the order on September 30, 2026 and released it on October 1. The Federal Register published it on October 26 (91 FR 67214), which set November 25, 2026 as the effective date, as the FCC confirmed in Public Notice DA 26-1159. The new text of 47 CFR § 64.1200(a)(10) replaces a portion of the rule whose effective date had been delayed to January 31, 2027.

RuleWhat the amended text says
Exclusive opt-out methodCallers may designate one of three methods: an automated voice or key-press opt-out on the call; a reply text with a standardized word; or a website or phone number the caller sets up for opt-outs. Other methods then need not be processed.
DisclosureThe designated method must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously on the call or in the text. For reply texts, one standardized word in each text is enough.
Standardized words"stop", "quit", "end", "revoke", "opt out", "cancel", "unsubscribe". A sender that uses reply texts must accept any of them.
Scope of an opt-outAn opt-out from an informational call or text may apply only to that category of informational messages. An opt-out from telemarketing stops all telemarketing calls and texts from that caller.
DeadlineOpt-outs must be honored within a reasonable time, no later than 10 business days after receipt.
Confirmation textA text that only confirms the opt-out, with no marketing, is allowed; sent within five minutes, it is presumed to fall within the consumer's prior consent.
No-reply textsIf the texting protocol does not allow replies, each text must say so and give other reasonable ways to opt out.
No designated methodCallers that do not designate a method must keep processing opt-outs made by any reasonable means.

The order also lets financial institutions send fraud alerts to wireless numbers obtained from a "reliable source", and delegates a plain-language rewrite of the robocall rules to FCC staff without changing their substance.

Why it matters for AI calls and automated follow-up

The rules apply to calls and texts that need consent under the FCC's robocall rules, including calls that use an artificial or prerecorded voice. In a February 2024 declaratory ruling (FCC 24-17), the FCC confirmed that this term covers "current AI technologies that generate human voices". An AI voice agent that calls U.S. leads back, and automated texts such as reminders and follow-ups, sit inside these rules.

For a service business the change is mostly operational. The opt-out method has to be written into every template, every channel has to recognise the same seven words, and an opt-out has to reach every system that sends messages: the texting tool, the CRM sequences and the voice agent.

What is still open

The FCC also asked, in a further notice of proposed rulemaking (91 FR 67239), whether callers should have to offer a one-step way to opt out of all future robocalls. Comments are due November 25, 2026 and replies December 28, 2026.

Canadian businesses

The FCC rules govern calls and texts to U.S. numbers. A Canadian firm with U.S. clients or U.S. phone numbers on its lead list should check its templates against them with counsel. Texts to Canadian numbers fall under CASL, which has its own identification, unsubscribe and 10-business-day rules; our guide to missed call text back in Canada walks through them. A five-point check for your follow-up stack

  1. List every automated channel that reaches U.S. numbers: texts, voice agents, reminder calls.
  2. Pick the opt-out method for each and add it to every template.
  3. Test that all seven standardized words stop the sequence.
  4. Check that an opt-out in one tool reaches the CRM and the others.
  5. Tag each message as informational or telemarketing, since opt-outs now differ by category.

Questions business owners are asking

When do the new FCC opt-out rules take effect?

On November 25, 2026. The FCC adopted the order (FCC 26-67) on September 30, released it on October 1, and the Federal Register published it on October 26, 2026.

Do I have to include all seven opt-out words in every text?

No. If a reply text is your designated opt-out method, one standardized word such as STOP in each text meets the disclosure duty. You must still accept any of the seven words: stop, quit, end, revoke, opt out, cancel, unsubscribe.

Do these rules cover AI voice calls?

The rules apply to calls with an artificial or prerecorded voice. In its February 2024 declaratory ruling (FCC 24-17), the FCC confirmed that this term covers AI technologies that generate human voices.

Does this apply to Canadian businesses?

The FCC rules govern calls and texts to U.S. numbers. Canadian businesses that call or text U.S. clients should check them with counsel. Texts to Canadian numbers fall under CASL.
