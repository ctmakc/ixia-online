Canada's 2027–2029 Immigration Levels Plan: what it means for immigration consultants' intake

On November 5, 2026, Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) released the 2027–2029 Immigration Levels Plan. It keeps permanent resident admissions at 380,000 a year and new temporary resident arrivals at 370,000 a year. Alongside the plan, IRCC introduced a time-limited measure that adds admission space in 2027 to help up to 200,000 people already living in Canada move to permanent residence. For RCICs and immigration law practices, the plan changes who will be writing in over the next months and what the first reply has to ask.

What IRCC announced on November 5

The plan sets targets for three years. According to the supplementary information published by IRCC, targets for 2028 and 2029 are notional and will be confirmed or adjusted each year. The 2027 targets that matter most for consultants:

French-speaking admissions outside Quebec are set at 9.5% of permanent resident admissions in 2027, 10.5% in 2028 and 12% in 2029. IRCC's backgrounder also says the Rural Community Immigration Pilot becomes permanent and that planned admissions under the Provincial Nominee Program and the Atlantic Immigration Program increase.

The new measure: up to 200,000 people already in Canada

The time-limited measure adds admission space in 2027 for two cohorts of people who already live in Canada:

  1. Workers cohort, up to 100,000 people plus their families. Provinces and territories will receive additional Provincial Nominee Program space to nominate workers who meet regional needs. They can also identify workers who have already applied under select federal programs: the Atlantic Immigration Program and programs closed to new applications, including the Start-up Visa and Self-Employed Persons programs, the Agri-Food Pilot and the home care worker programs.
  2. Humanitarian cohort, up to 100,000 people. IRCC aims to process the remaining applications under the Hong Kong permanent residence pathways and the Ukraine Family Reunification Pathway, finalize applications already found eligible on humanitarian and compassionate grounds, and process applications from spouses, common-law partners and dependent children of Canadians and permanent residents who already live in Canada. IRCC expects this to bring wait times for the spouse or common-law partner class closer to 12 months for people destined outside Quebec.

The measure runs alongside the one-time initiatives from Budget 2025, which IRCC says will move up to 115,000 protected persons in Canada with their in-Canada dependants and 33,000 work permit holders to permanent residence in 2026 and 2027. The November 5 documents describe the cohorts and the programs involved. Check IRCC's program pages for application steps before advising a client.

What changes in a consultant's inbox

Announcements like this one can bring a wave of short messages from people who are already in Canada: "Does the 200,000 measure apply to me?", "My Start-up Visa file is pending, what happens now?", "Will my spousal sponsorship move faster?". Each message looks the same in an inbox and needs a different next step.

Two things follow for practices that live on inbound inquiries:

  1. Every inquiry needs a clear next step. A short reply with a consultation slot moves the question from the inbox to your calendar.
  2. Pending applications deserve a fast lane. Someone with an Atlantic Immigration Program, Start-up Visa, Self-Employed or Agri-Food Pilot file already has documents, dates and a history with IRCC. Intake that captures the program and the filing date on first contact lets the consultant prepare before the call.

A 30-day intake checklist for RCICs and immigration lawyers

  1. Add three required fields to every web form and chat: current status in Canada, province or territory of residence, and any pending permanent residence application (program and filing date).
  2. Create tags for the three routes in the diagram (worker, family in Canada, humanitarian) and one for "other pathways", so you can count demand by route each week.
  3. Reply to every new inquiry within minutes with a short acknowledgement and a booking link. After hours, the acknowledgement says when a consultant will follow up.
  4. Write one plain-language page on your site that summarizes the November 5 plan and links to IRCC's documents. Update it when IRCC publishes application details.
  5. Keep eligibility answers with licensed staff. Automated replies confirm receipt, collect facts and book a time. The answer on eligibility comes from the consultant at the consultation.
  6. Start a follow-up sequence for people who are waiting for IRCC details, so they hear from you when the instructions are out.

Questions consultants are asking

What are Canada's immigration targets for 2027?

380,000 permanent resident admissions and 370,000 new temporary resident arrivals (220,000 workers and 150,000 students), according to the 2027–2029 Immigration Levels Plan released on November 5, 2026.

Who is in the 200,000 time-limited measure?

People already living in Canada, in two cohorts: up to 100,000 workers and their families, nominated through additional provincial space or identified from select federal programs, and up to 100,000 people in a humanitarian cohort that includes spouses, partners and children of Canadians and permanent residents who already live in Canada.
