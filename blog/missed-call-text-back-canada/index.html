Missed call text back for Canadian service businesses: what to send, how fast, and what CASL allows

Missed call text back sends an automatic text to anyone whose call your business did not answer. The text confirms the call was seen and offers one next step: a booking link, a callback time or a reply option. For a clinic, a law office or a trades business, it keeps the enquiry with you while the caller still has the phone in hand. In Canada, these texts fall under CASL; texts to U.S. numbers also fall under FCC rules that change on November 25, 2026.

Published November 6, 2026 · 7 min read

Short version: send the text within one to two minutes of the missed call, name your business, give one clear next step, and include a way to opt out. Keep the first text a direct reply to the call, with no promotion. Route the reply to a person for anything urgent, sensitive or priced. This is operational guidance; for a ruling on your own messages, ask counsel.

Why the first minutes after a missed call matter

A caller who reaches voicemail can try the next number on the list. A 2011 Harvard Business Review study of 2,241 US companies found that firms which tried to contact a lead within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify it as firms that waited longer. A text that arrives while the caller is still looking keeps your business in the conversation until someone can call back.

What a good first text contains

The first text has one job: get the caller to the next step without a second phone call. Five parts cover it.

PartExample wordingWhy it is there
Who is texting"Hi, this is [business name]."The caller knows the number; the business name confirms it. CASL also asks commercial messages to identify the sender.
Acknowledgement"Sorry we missed your call."Shows the call was seen, so the caller does not need to try again.
One next step"Book a time here: [link] or reply with a good time to call."One choice keeps the reply to a tap or a word.
When a person follows up"We call back during business hours: [hours]."Sets the expectation for after-hours callers.
Opt-out"Reply STOP to opt out."Required for commercial messages under CASL and, for U.S. numbers, the disclosure the FCC asks for.

Keep the text free of case, health or account details: the caller may share the phone, and those details belong on a call or a secure form.

What CASL says about texting someone who just called

Canada's anti-spam law (CASL, S.C. 2010, c. 23) defines an electronic message as one sent "by any means of telecommunication, including a text, sound, voice or image message", so business texts are in scope. The provisions that matter for a text-back:

ProvisionWhat the text saysWhat it means for a text-back
CASL s. 6(1)–(2)A commercial electronic message needs consent, express or implied, and must identify the sender, give contact information and include an unsubscribe mechanism.Name the business, keep the number reachable and offer STOP.
Regulations SOR/2013-221, s. 3(b)Section 6 does not apply to a commercial electronic message "sent in response to a request, inquiry or complaint or is otherwise solicited by the person to whom the message is sent".A direct reply to the caller's enquiry is the closest fit. The text does not mention missed calls by name.
CASL s. 10(10)(e)An existing business relationship, and so implied consent, arises from an inquiry or application made within the six months before the message is sent.Follow-ups on the same enquiry within six months can rely on implied consent, with sender ID and unsubscribe.
CASL s. 6(6)(a)A message that solely provides a quote or estimate the person requested does not need consent; it still needs sender ID and an unsubscribe mechanism.Sending a requested price range by text is covered, with the same identification.
CASL s. 11(3)An unsubscribe request must take effect without delay and no later than 10 business days after it is sent.STOP should stop the automation the same day.

Two practical rules follow. Keep the first text a reply to the call: no promotion, no offer for another service. And include the sender name and STOP in every text, so each message meets section 6 even if a court reads the inquiry exclusion narrowly. Ask counsel before adding marketing content or texting past six months.

Texting U.S. numbers: the FCC rules change on November 25

If your callers include U.S. numbers, U.S. telemarketing law also applies to automated texts. Amended FCC rules (FCC 26-67, 91 FR 67214) take effect on November 25, 2026. Senders may designate a reply with a standardized word such as STOP as the exclusive opt-out method, must disclose it in each text, and must honor opt-outs within 10 business days. Where text-back fits in the intake chain

A text-back closes one gap. The enquiry still needs an owner after the reply arrives. Three links decide whether it turns into a booking:

  1. Routing. Replies go to the person who books, with the call time and number attached.
  2. Escalation. Urgent, sensitive or price-specific replies go to a person the same day; the automation does not answer them.
  3. Logging. Every missed call and its outcome lands in the CRM as its own source, so you can see how many calls you miss and how many come back.

Questions about missed call text back

What is missed call text back?

An automatic text message sent to a caller whose call went unanswered. It confirms the call was seen and gives the caller a next step, usually a booking link or a callback time, so the enquiry stays with your business.

Does CASL allow texting back someone who called my business?

CASL covers text messages. Its regulations exclude a commercial electronic message sent in response to a request, inquiry or complaint from the consent and content rules of section 6. The Act does not mention missed calls by name, so a careful setup keeps the first text to a direct reply, identifies the business and offers an opt-out. Ask counsel how it applies to your case.

Can I keep texting a caller after the first reply?

Under CASL, an inquiry made to your business creates implied consent for six months. Commercial messages sent on that basis must identify the sender, give contact details and include an unsubscribe mechanism, and an unsubscribe must take effect within 10 business days.

What changes on November 25, 2026 for texts to U.S. numbers?

Amended FCC rules (FCC 26-67) take effect. Senders may designate an exclusive opt-out method, such as replying with a standardized word like STOP, must disclose it in each text, and must honor opt-outs within 10 business days.
